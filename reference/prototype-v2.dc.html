Sources and tools

JPL Horizons — reference for Sun and Earth positions. LROC Quickmap — maps and average illumination for any site. NASA Moon Trek — 3D terrain. CSA Moon exploration — Canada's lunar missions. What this page adds: a site × landing-date calendar that answers “the launch slipped — where and when now?” in one view. Sites: IM-1, IM-2, Chandrayaan-3, Apollo 11 from LROC landing-site images. Artemis regions are 15 × 15 km; NASA has not published centre points, so each point here is one we picked inside the region. Use of AI: this page, its calculation code and the ephemeris script were written with Claude (Anthropic) during the hackathon. Site coordinates were researched with AI help and checked against LROC. Design choices were reviewed with AI critiques.

{{cMsg}}
Why is the Sun so low?The Moon's axis is tilted only 1.5°. At 84°S the Sun never rises more than about 8°, and closer to the pole even less. Press “True scale” to see how flat that is.
Why does Earth move?Earth stays in almost the same part of the sky, but the Moon's wobble (libration) swings it about 7° up, down and sideways every month. Near the pole that is enough to drop it below the horizon.
What is not modelled yetTerrain (next step: horizons from NASA LOLA data), ground-station schedules and relays. The Sun counts as up when its centre is above the horizon — then half of its disc is visible.
