The problem

At the lunar south pole, even a small change in timing can change a mission

  • Ridges can block the Sun for hours or even days.
  • Libration moves Earth by about 7° — it can drop behind the terrain.

A lander needs sunlight for power and Earth in view for a direct link. Ideally both, from touchdown.

Checked on a smooth Moon: this validates the Sun and Earth directions, not the terrain. The constant +0.0055° in the Sun's azimuth is aberration of light, which Horizons applies.

Known limitations
  • Each site is a single point, not a region: at the Bussey 2010 point our model gives 60% Sun; a ridge pixel 2.1 km away gives 86%.
  • The 240 m terrain cannot resolve ridges under ~500 m; rise and set times may be off by several hours.
  • Line of sight only: no ground stations, relay satellites, slopes or thermal limits.
  • Sites north of 75°S and your own points use a smooth Moon; positions every 3 hours.
Validated within the tested conditions — not a flight-certified mission-planning tool.6 / 7
